Version Seven — Notes From My Notebook

Hi — it’s Yumi. I read Version Seven.pdf alongside the two manuscripts and wrote down how it changes the way I think about 教育ローマ字 (Kyouro). This is not a line‑by‑line summary; it’s what I’ll carry into my own spelling and reading, with pointers so you can check me.

Boundaries I can see and feel

I like that token‑internal AP boundaries are now explicit and spaced, with ʻ for an active fall and ’ for a latent one (Version Seven). It matches the spirit of the book where boundary behavior is something you should be able to test, not guess. When I place a fall, I’ll ask the same question I ask with the nF rule in the book: does the condition actually hold here? If not, I don’t “expect” it to surface. Having the accent sit inside ʻ or ’ when they touch is a small typographic rule, but it helps me keep strings readable when I practice.

Stress as a readable consequence

Stress being tied to final R of an AP (Version Seven) clicks with the way the main book ties prosody to structure. The IP rule (“second syllable if the first AP’s nF span is two or more moras, else the first”) gives me a concrete self‑check: if I predict a stress location that disagrees with this, I look for a wrong AP or a misapplied nF span. I also appreciate the honesty around “hollow” stress (like sentence‑final ne): it tells me not to over‑read semantics into something that’s mechanically stressed. The $‑diacritics are new to write, but the idea of marking what can/can’t carry stress within a token feels consistent with the book’s habit of externalizing analysis.

Small marks that reduce guessing

Switching the “syllabicity dot” to a diaeresis, and giving ä/ë when h is not pronounced as h and the following vowel is a/e (Version Seven), will probably save me time when typing from ear. I don’t have to remember a special dot; I can lean on a familiar mark that tells me “start of a nucleus here.” The stance on wu (no longer recommended) also helps: the book’s romanization table had already nudged me to be careful with う; now I’ll favor the deletion‑plus‑diaeresis path instead of writing wu and second‑guessing myself later.

Why this feels like the same project

Even with Seven’s new surface pieces (ʻ and ’, diaereses, $‑marks), the skeleton hasn’t changed for me: predict prosody from structure and write that prediction into the string. The center of gravity is still the book’s core rules, like the nF assignment inside an AP (book: the mora after an accent and all to the left get nF), and the stated environments where nF can extend (‑kara at 0, ‑no at 1 and not at the left edge). My habit is to keep those open when I read anything new.

What I’ll practice next

  • Boundary hygiene: Re‑type a few lines with ʻ and ’ plus spacing, checking whether the “active only at the right edge” condition is truly met in each case (Version Seven).
  • Stress diaries: Take snippets and mark $‑diacritics; if I ever place ˆ, ´, `, ˘ in a way that contradicts the IP rule, I’ll trace it back to an AP decision.
  • Diaeresis muscle memory: Practice ü, ï after vowels or between voiceless consonant letters, and ä, ë when h is not pronounced as h before a/e; keep it consistent so I don’t hesitate mid‑typing.
